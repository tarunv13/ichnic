# How Ichnic speaks

Ichnic writes for three readers at once: someone who arrived from a search and has thirty seconds,
a researcher or official who will check every number, and an answer engine that will quote a
sentence out of context. These rules keep all three well served. They draw on the communication
recommendations of the Oxford Agile Initiative's *Environment and National Security: Exploring the
Risk Pathways* (2026, section 11) and on what the Atlas has learned from its readers.

## Seven rules

1. **Answer first, then show the evidence, then the limit.** Lead with the finding in one sentence,
   give the number that supports it, and state what the number cannot tell you. Every page has all
   three, in that order.
2. **A tangible hook, then the system.** Start from something concrete: a seizure, a route, a species.
   Then show the pathway it belongs to. Local examples without the system mislead; the system without
   an example is too abstract to care about.
3. **Uncertainty as a range and a reason.** Say how strong the evidence is (official, corroborated,
   single report; strong, partial, a gap) and why, never just "uncertain". Distinguish doubt about a
   detail from confidence that the pattern exists.
4. **Urgency with a way forward.** Pair every risk with something that can be done and someone who can
   do it. Alarm without agency produces fatalism.
5. **Reported, not happened.** Counts measure reporting and inspection effort as well as crime. Say so
   wherever a number could be read as a measure of trade.
6. **Security without militarisation.** Wildlife crime is a security risk, and a security frame has
   also been used to justify harm to people who live alongside wildlife. Count enforcement; never
   glorify it. Never name an accused person. Treat harms to communities as part of the pathway.
7. **Plain words, exact numbers.** Short sentences, active verbs, no jargon without a gloss, no
   superlatives the data cannot carry. Numbers in text, not only in charts, so they can be quoted.

## Words

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| seized, reported, recorded | caught, busted, smashed |
| people arrested (a count) | names, photographs, "kingpin" |
| the open evidence is thin | "no evidence" (when evidence is only closed) |
| shares a map with | caused, linked to (for co-location) |
| trade in plants and animals | "exotic", "species" alone for plants |

## Pictures

A chart or diagram earns its place when it shows a relationship the text would take a paragraph to
explain. Every figure has a sentence beside it that says what to see, numbers in text for the key
values, and colours that keep their meaning across the Atlas: cases red, species green, trade amber,
routes blue; strong, partial and gap evidence in green, amber and red.
